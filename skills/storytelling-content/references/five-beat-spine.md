# Five-Beat Spine

## Contents

## The five beats

Use this as a planning scaffold, not mandatory published phrasing.

1. **Prior state:** who or what existed before the relevant change?
2. **Repeated normal:** what pattern kept the old state stable?
3. **Disruption:** what specific event or realization broke the pattern?
4. **Consequences:** what happened because of the disruption, step by step?
5. **Resulting state:** what is different now, including unresolved costs?

The spine may be one sentence per beat for short content or a section per beat
for long content. Do not inflate a topic into a story when no event, decision,
conflict, or change exists; use explanatory structure instead.

## Finding transformation

Write it as `before -> pressure/choice -> after`. The after-state may be:

- a result;
- a changed belief;
- a new constraint;
- a failed attempt with a precise lesson;
- a question that became sharper rather than solved.

Prefer the smallest transformation the evidence supports.

## Expanding the spine

- Split consequences into a causal chain only when each link is supported.
- Turn one decisive beat into a scene only when concrete details are known.
- Use a nested five-beat spine for a meaningful sub-event, not every tangent.
- Compress background that does not alter tension, choice, or understanding.

## Weak-spine diagnostics

- **No disruption:** the piece is a list wearing story language.
- **No consequence:** the disruption has no narrative weight.
- **No change:** the ending repeats the opening state.
- **False victory:** uncertainty or cost disappears for emotional neatness.
- **Chronology dump:** every event receives equal space regardless of function.
